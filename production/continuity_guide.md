# Continuity guide — RETURN TO THE SLOPE

**Status:** Stage 1 proposed continuity rules. Character and architecture are not yet generated or approved.

## Master skier — proposed, pending approval

- Male, approximately 32; lean, powerful athletic build; realistic weathered skin; grey-blue eyes; subtle eyebrow scar.
- Dark navy technical jacket and pants, restrained orange accents, matte black helmet, dark mirrored goggles, black gloves, believable ski boots and alpine skis.
- Do not generate until Stage 3. Once approved, designate a single approved master reference and preserve it; never silently replace it. Record the approved reference filename/ID and any accepted variations in the asset manifest.
- Keep facial landmarks, scar, age, build, proportions, hair visibility, kit construction/colors, helmet, goggles, gloves, boots, and ski design stable. Injury-to-return progression changes emotion and performance confidence, not identity or unexplained wardrobe.
- In rehab scenes, the athlete may remove helmet/goggles as motivated by location; maintain recognizable face and scar. Ski equipment remains only where physically appropriate.

## Architecture and locations

- Stage 2's approved exterior is the visual source of truth. Do not generate a competing exterior without explicit approval.
- Keep stone base, dark timber/vertical slats, charcoal steel, glazing rhythm, roofline, entrance, railings, scale, snow conditions, and mountain orientation stable.
- Interiors should plausibly belong to that building: related timber/steel/stone palette, window proportions, warm practical light, and credible circulation. Avoid changing the center into unrelated generic clinic spaces.
- No signage, facility name, or logo in generated images; typeset verified branding in post.

## Screen direction and time

- Establish the opening slope's travel direction and preserve the action axis across the fall and comeback, unless a clear establishing shot motivates reversal.
- Dawn/sun direction and snow condition should remain coherent for the narrative pair of ski scenes. If the return intentionally occurs later, make the progression apparent and preserve recognizable terrain.
- Cold exterior light / warm interior shift is an emotional transition, not a filter jump; protect skin and snow detail.

## Clinical and equipment continuity

- Never imply a named product or service unless verified. Named SkyTech simulator use requires an actual verified reference; no design invention.
- Validate equipment against supplied references and clinical review. Sensor placement, belts/harnesses, screens, pool supervision, clothing, and patient posture must make physical sense.
- Monitors and interfaces should have graphics and patient data replaced or added in post. No random text or real patient information.
- Team composition/credentials and any apparent clinical intervention must be approved and factually accurate. No white coats by default; use approved professional sports-medical clothing.

## Asset, generation, and review discipline

For each asset, record ID/filename, purpose, status, approvals, source/reference dependencies, prompt version, and known limitations in `asset_manifest.md`. Reuse approved assets; do not overwrite an approved reference without explicit authorization. Review anatomy, motion, materials, architecture, lighting, identity, artifacts, text/logos, and cross-shot continuity before approval. Keep final type/logo in post. Do not advance to the next production gate until the user approves the current one.
