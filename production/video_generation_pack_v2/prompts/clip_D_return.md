## Positive prompt (paste as one block)

> One single continuous shot, no cuts, no camera jump. Photorealistic cinematic alpine footage, 16:9, 24 fps, natural winter daylight with a soft high overcast and one lifted break of brighter light on the ridge, cool snow shadows, deep alpine blue and white palette, subtle film grain.
>
> SUBJECT — preserve exactly the man in the reference images: same face, light stubble, deep navy technical ski jacket with restrained orange zipper pulls, dark charcoal ski pants, matte black helmet with dark reflective goggles now worn over the eyes, black gloves, black ski boots, the same skis and poles. Same identity, same kit, same equipment as in the opening shot, no logos, no badges.
>
> PLACE — the same Altai slope and the same camera side as the opening shot, so the return echoes the fall: deep powder over a firm base, snow-dusted conifers, distant rocky ridgeline. He travels from upper right toward lower left, camera on the downhill front-quarter side of the action, never crossing the axis.
>
> ACTION, in this order, as one unbroken take:
> 1. (first ~15%) He enters frame already moving at moderate speed, weight centred, in control.
> 2. (~15–45%) He links one clean carved turn: knees flexed, hips angulated over the outside ski, edges engaging, a sculpted plume of powder thrown from the ski tails, upper body quiet and confident.
> 3. (~45–75%) He transitions smoothly and holds a second, slightly longer arc, shoulders facing down the fall line, both hands forward, poles carried with intent, powder following the edges in a clean arc.
> 4. (final ~25%) He exits the second turn and carries speed down-slope with the slope opening out beneath him, snow spray settling, the camera easing with him and letting the terrain and ridgeline fill the frame.
>
> Confident, skilled, believable skiing — the work of a competent recreational skier at moderate speed. Not a stunt, not a race, not superhuman, no jump, no airborne skis, no extreme edge angle, no spraying wall of snow.
>
> CAMERA — one continuous camera: wide-to-medium tracking from the downhill front-quarter, smooth parallel motion with a gentle push, subtle parallax against the conifers, settled horizon. No cuts, no orbit, no whip pan, no snap zoom, no drone acrobatics.
>
> PHYSICS — real edge grip on soft snow, believable weight transfer through both turns, poles used naturally, powder with mass, garment and helmet strap movement in the wind.

## Notes for the operator

- This is the shot that must match the opening axis exactly. Check the descent direction (upper right → lower left) and the camera side before accepting a take; a mirrored clip breaks continuity against Clip A and the stills.
- If a take ends on an empty slope, that is useful — the approved end card is typeset afterwards in post, and the last frames can be extended with a still of the mountain.
- Do not accept a take where the second turn ends in a fall or a stumble: this is the confidence payoff.
- Keep the goggles *over the eyes* here (as in `REF-SCENE-09`), not pushed up as in the aftermath stills.
